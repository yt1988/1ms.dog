How it works

How fast is Shopee from your network?

To compare, the home page tests Shopee together with the other Local favorites services: Lazada, Grab, LINE.

What is an exit IP?

It's the address websites see when you visit them. At home it usually belongs to your internet provider. On a VPN, proxy, work network or iCloud Private Relay, it belongs to whoever forwards your traffic, and that can be in another city or another country. Services pick which server to send you to based on this address, so it affects your speed too.

Why do I see a direct exit and a proxy exit?

Because many proxies and VPNs use split routing: they choose the route by website, so some sites go direct and the rest through the proxy. One IP check only sees its own route. So we ask several at once: 1ms.dog itself, a site in mainland China, Cloudflare, ip.sb, ipinfo.io, IPv4-only and IPv6-only checks, and WebRTC (over UDP). We group what they see by network and mark each exit as direct or proxy. If services that are usually blocked in your country answer anyway, we tell you there's an exit none of the checks could see.

How is latency measured?

Your browser opens a real HTTPS connection to each service. The first request only sets up the connection (DNS lookup, TCP and TLS handshakes); it's shown separately and not counted. Then the same connection is reused for a few timed requests, and we show the median. So you see one round trip from your device to the service's nearest server, through your Wi-Fi, provider and any proxy: the delay your apps actually feel.

Why is this different from ping?

Ping uses ICMP, which browsers can't send, and many networks treat ICMP differently from normal traffic, so a good ping doesn't always mean a fast app. HTTPS timing takes the same path as the apps you use. The numbers are usually a few milliseconds higher than ping, because the server has to answer a request.

What do the dog speeds mean?

Zoomies ≤ 30 ms: about the same city. Sprint ≤ 80 ms: same region. Trot ≤ 150 ms: fine for almost everything. Stroll ≤ 300 ms: you'll notice it in games and calls. Nap > 300 ms: far away, or a congested route. Not back: no connection within 5 seconds, so the service can't be reached from here. Under 2 ms unlocks the legendary 1ms dog.

Is my data sent anywhere?

Your results aren't sent to us. Latency requests go straight from your browser to the services being tested. To find your exit IP, your browser asks 1ms.dog and a few public IP lookup services, and they see your IP, because that's how an IP check works. Your last result stays in this browser so the dog can tell you if anything changed. A result leaves your browser only when you share it or report a problem. A shared link carries the country and timings, plus city and network only if you tick the box, and never IP addresses. On 1ms.dog the link is shortened and a picture of the result is stored for the link preview in chat apps.
